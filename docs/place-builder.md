# Place builder

`apps/playground/tools/place-build.mts` (registered as `hitreg.place-build`, `tools/place-build/`) builds a small or
medium outdoor place from DATA: a place archetype, the zone brief's place, its reservation and the measured ground.
The agent picks the archetype and a few overrides; the tool lays the pads, places catalogued structures, sweeps the
walls, lays the footpaths, clears the ground, writes the first dressing plans and runs every install gate. Hero
places (a landmark that needs its own concept and bespoke geometry: a sea arch chapel, a waterfall stair) stay with
an Opus owner (`poi-creator`).

Geometry is deliberately simple (owner ruling 2026-10-19: WoW Classic / EverQuest): a pad, 1-3 catalogued
structures, one landmark with `fogReach`, a few dressing areas and clearings per pocket. Strong textures do the work.

## Archetypes

`projects/world-kit/authoring/place-archetypes/<id>.json`, validated by `placeArchetypeSchema`
(`apps/playground/tools/_place-archetype.mts`; every field is `.describe()`d there). Shipped: `camp`, `croft-row`,
`shrine`, `graveyard`, `beach-landing`, `work-yard`, `den`, `watch-ruin`, `halt-yard`, `cuttings`, `castle`, `lone-tower`
(`place-build archetypes` lists them). An archetype is a core pocket (the landmark) and up to four more pockets placed
at `at x spread[tier]` in the place frame (+z toward the approach). Each pocket: a pad, structures (slots with a
catalogued prefab and `choices`), walls (the `wall` operator: ring, square, line), a dressing area with a first plan
of props BY NAME, a creature clearing. `propRequests` keep a spot open for what the catalogue lacks.

Overrides: `--set core.marker=parish-weep/weep-lych-gate` swaps a slot's prefab; `--set spread.medium=100`,
`--set core.radius=12` set fields. The sets are saved in the job's `brief.json` and re-used by the next `plan`.

## Commands (from apps/playground, each inside the heavy-job lock)

```
T="node --max-old-space-size=6144 node_modules/tsx/dist/cli.mjs tools/place-build.mts"
$T plan  <world> --zone <z> --location <id> [--archetype a] [--set k=v ...] [--at x,z | --at auto] [--faces E | auto] [--roam 1.5]   # job dir, never the world
$T all   <world> --zone <z> --location <id> [--shots]                         # plan -> lab -> build -> gates (-> shots only with --shots: no per-place shots while building)
$T build|gates|shots <world> --zone <z> --location <id> [--only lips,float]    # one stage again
$T approach <world> --zone <z> --location <id>                               # re-plan the APPROACH only (recorded site, no search): track in; old footpath ribbons migrated to tracks; then lab, build, gates, install
$T tracks <world> --zone <z> --location <id>                                 # MIGRATE only: an old job's footpath ribbons -> carved + painted footpath tracks on the same lines (no re-plan, no build)
$T dress <world> --zone <z> --location <id>                                  # re-lay the DRESSING only (vignettes by pocket role, from the LIVE plans: swaps and quest objects kept); then lab, build, gates, install
$T props <world> --zone <z> --location <id>                                  # the layout's hero-prop sheet (reports/layout-props.png, no browser)
$T evidence <world> --zone <z> --location <id>                               # progress.json evidence.readShot from taken shots
$T scale <world> --zone <z> --location <id> [--live]                        # a PAINTED place vs its painting (concept-marks.json), in the painting's pixels
$T install <world> --zone <z> --location <id> [--dry-run | --uninstall]        # coordinator, after a fresh review; geometry gates only
$T build|install <world> --zone <z> --location <id> --draft [--draft-note "<dated go>"]   # a half-finished place AS IT IS (owner's dated go): no review stage asked, every geometry guard kept (below)
$T render-batch <world> --zone <z> --places a,b,c [--port p] [--night auto|off] [--extra <spots.json>] [--dry-run]   # ONE live-world render over a batch after the install
$T quality <world> --zone <z> [--shots] [--only a,b] [--port p]              # every installed place of the zone graded in the LIVE world
$T quality <world> --zone <z> --labs --only a,b                                # built, not installed: each graded on its own lab scene -> <zone>/reports/poi-quality-labs.md
```

`render-batch` (Derek 2026-10-31: no per-place shot runs while building; install in batches, then one render job; `tools/_render-batch.mts`):
ONE light-shots run over the LIVE world (one browser, the world loaded once, shaders warm) inside the render lock
(`tools/blender-lock.mjs` FIFO on `.hitreg/render.lock`). Per place: iso-ne, iso-sw (review-only, fog off, noon) first, then
core-close (else core-front), the read view of the approach its footpath joins, and the views marked `"batch": true` in
views.json (max 2; none marked = its first fixed camera named `*-close`); 20:00 too only where the live place has lights
(`burnsAtDusk`, handoff `read.duskLight`). `--extra <spots.json>` spots ride along (review-only). A stalled spot is skipped
and listed (`--spot-timeout`, default 300 s); a run that dies is retried once for the spots it never reached. Pose check: every
frame's recorded camera and body must stand within 6 m of its own spot (`poseFaults`); a frame that does not is dropped and
its spot re-shot once (`index.json` `poseFaults`). `index.json` `spotTiming` has each spot's travel / settle / frames seconds
and any settle that hit `--settle-max`. Out: each place's
frames in `<job>/evidence/live/<batch>/` + its record `<job>/evidence/live/<batch>.json` (light-shots run shape, `washVerdict`,
`rows`; only the latest batch is kept per place) and its read shot in progress.json; one contact sheet per place
`<zone>/reports/batches/<batch>/sheet-<place>.png` (iso pair on top) + `index.json` (place, frames, skipped, rows).

DRAFT install (`--draft`, Derek 2026-11-03: "bring them in live as they are and we can alter them"): `plan --draft` runs only on a job holding the owner's `layout.json` (expands it as it is; w-temple5), `build --draft` builds
the lab without design-review (the design gate is printed, not refused); `install --draft` asks for no review stage (final
review, design, size, art, scale, rock skins, dress stay OPEN, never faked) but refuses on any geometry fault: job.mjs
`draftInstallBlock` (float, water edges, walk, flat art, monument approach, the 6 m rule, and every `DRAFT_GEOMETRY_GATES`
row of the lab's current gates table: fine, roads, lips, scatter-float, float, seated, shelters, abut, water-edges, zfight,
op-check, support, walk), the ground guard, and the overlap check (`reports/draft-overlap.json`: a lab piece within 3 m of
another place's or the town's live piece, deeper than 0.6 x the radius inside a live place's reservation, inside a town disc,
or on a road tread the place does not own). The live root gets the tag `draft`, `installed` records the note, progress.json
`draftInstalls` keeps what was open. Finishing a draft = the normal stages, then a plain `install` (it replaces the draft).

SHOT rows: wash, the read shot, the design's sample frames (gate 16) and see-through (gate 8) are judged from the latest
batch evidence, never on the install path (`job.mjs installBlock` asks for them only with `shots: true`; `SHOT_ROWS`). In
`gates`, `wash` and the `place` row's pictures read the batch record when it is newer than the ops, else a lab shots run newer
than the ops, else they are DEFERRED (`def`, ok); `design` runs without the sample. A batch fault is a fix round.

`quality` writes `<zone>/reports/poi-quality.md` (+ .json), weakest first, on four measured lines (`tools/_place-quality.mts`):
the core's built footprint across vs 1.5 x its tier's core floor, loose props per 100 m2 of the core area, walls in the
terrain cliff tile, and the landmark's (or smoke column's) pixel height in the noon read shot (floor `READ_PX_MIN` 16 px on
the 1280 x 720, 60 deg frame). `--shots` first re-takes core-close (core-front on older jobs), aerial-150 and the read view of the joined approach for
every place in ONE light-shots run (`<zone>/reports/poi-quality/<world>/<place>--<view>-12.png`).

Zone defaults: `<zone>/place-build.json` `{ "builtStone": { "face", "cap", "footing" } }` = the material ids every
place-build wall (and a skirt whose structure's own base material is no material asset) takes; `plan` refuses ids
missing from the closure.

A pinned core (`--at`) ignores the brief's `site.faces` (it described another spot) and faces the nearest approach point 40 m+ away, which can be the OTHER approach's end: `--faces <compass>` names the front (kept in brief.json). A landmark owner writes `<job>/contract.json` `{ idea, mustRead[], views[] }` BEFORE the grey box (HERO-BRIDGE discipline: one idea, 3 fixed cameras incl. the 300 m road view, `camera` + `target` each); every `plan` merges those views into `views.json` and `shots` takes them.

Scale (gate 17, Red Dial 2026-10-30: "you don't have the scale correct with the concept art"): a place with an approved painting (`<zone>/concept/<id>.site.json`) writes `<job>/concept-marks.json` `{ view, marks: [{ id, pieces[], box: [x0,y0,x1,y1], dims }] }` — each hero piece's (or group's) box in the painting's pixels; `scale` projects the built pieces through the painting's own camera (vfov 60) and fails any judged width/height more than 15 % off, naming the factor (`reports/scale.json`, a gates row, read by the install guard). Judge `w` only where the painting hides a piece's foot; a group (ring + plinth) measures a silhouette whose ends are both visible. Fix PROPORTIONS, not one piece: the ring read small because the pillars were tall. EXTENT (Red Dial 2026-10-30, Derek: "the concept art is correct, it should be that grand"; every piece matched while the painted city was a ring and a few yards): `concept-marks.json` `extent` { view (e.g. `far`), anchor { pieces, box } (the hero's painted box: zoom = painted / built width, since a painting made over the true render may draw the hero and the city round it larger), outline [[px,py]...] (the painted city's ground edge), masses[] (reported, not judged), reach 12 } is back-projected through that camera (zoomed) onto the lab ground and compared with the BUILT DISTRICT (layout structures grown by `reach`: islands count their own disc). Built area or width (along the painting's right axis) more than 15 % short fails; a painted place with marks but no extent fails.

Support (gate `support`, Red Dial 2026-10-30, Derek: "braziers on top of pillars are not centred, or are hanging off edges and clipping through some geometry"): a layout piece `on` another names the host's `marker` (`fire-1`, read from the host's `<model>.markers.json`, so a re-exported kit piece moves its fires with it) or, with no marker and no `sink`, snaps to the centre of the host's top face when that face is a pedestal (at most `SUPPORT_SNAP_FACE` x the piece's footprint). The plan measures every such piece (`supportOf`): its base may not hang off the host's top (more than `SUPPORT_OVERHANG_SHARE` of its base points with no top within 0.2 m under them) and the host's mesh may not rise more than `SUPPORT_CLIP_M` over its underside. Rows in reports/plan.json `support`, the gate writes `reports/support.json`. Never hand-copy marker coordinates into a layout script.

Wash (gate `wash`, Red Dial 2026-10-30, Derek: "so washed out from how bright that lighting is"; the old far read "fires > 4 px at 450 m" was passed with `light.glowReach` 800 halos 10 m across): `shots` runs light-shots `--wash` (each frame again with the halos and lamp fog glow off) and `tools/_light-wash.mts` judges every frame against `WASH_BAND` (near/mid luma of the lower 55 %, blown-white share, glare share >= 200, and the share the halos + fog glow alone brighten by >= 20) and every fire >= 300 m away in a dusk frame by its FLAME (peak over the ground ring, warm): >= 75 % must read, their median glow radius <= 9 px (the nearest few light the haze round them; a halo model shows in all). The veil counts only what the frame taken again with the lights back on agrees on (a drifting cloud shadow is not a halo). A beacon reads by a bigger flame or a higher seat, never by `glow`/`glowReach` (the halo is capped at glow 1.5 and held to ~3 px past 300 m in the engine). Diagnose a washed frame with `light-shots --layers` (one frame per layer off: halos, pools, fog, shading, bloom). `reports/wash.json`. Far fires are judged out to 1200 m (`FAR_FIRE_MAX_M`, the flame's vfx cull; a light past it has no flame drawn). Only a fire the camera can SEE is judged: light-shots rays each fire light from the camera (`sightHits`, src/line-of-sight.ts) and a fire whose ray meets terrain or a mesh more than 4 m short of it (a pit wall, a rim, a hill) is `occluded` and not counted (Rustgut 2026-11-10). A blue-white light (b > r, a spirit fire) is judged by its own hue, taken as the 3x3 mean around its white-hot core. Particle flames sleep past 220 m, so a landmark beacon sets `vfx.farDraw` to keep its flame drawn out to its cullDistance (city-fires sun-beacon, city-beacon-great and moon-beacon). A small flame whose colour is lost far off (a cold flame's blue rim under a pixel at 300 m) adds `vfx.farGrow` (metres): past it the cloud keeps its screen size, and up close nothing changes (moon-beacon, 150). The gate fails when the evidence run is missing any `views.json` view, because light-shots skips a spot that stalls past `--spot-timeout`, and far views are the ones that stall.

The job dir is `zonegen/<world>/zones/<z>/pois/<id>/`: `handoff.json` (routes for poi-walk, `siteDressing`, measured
size, landmark, viewpoints), `ops.json`, `world-ops.json` (pads), `operators.json` (walls, footpaths),
`clearings.json`, `encounter-clearings.json`, `dressing/<id>-<pocket>.json`, `views.json`, `prop-requests.json`,
`reports/` (`plan.json`, one report per gate, `gates.json` = the table) and `evidence/` (noon and dusk sheets).
The lab is `projects/pb-<id>/`, remade from the live world and scene by `all` / `lab`. A Sonnet dresser refines the
plans in `dressing/`; `build` re-applies them.
The lab is a CROP, not a copy (Derek 2026-10-30, "take a few chunks and send it to do the work there"; `tools/_lab-crop.mts`):
its box is every point the job touches plus every view camera (a fixed camera reaches 160 m past its target) plus 96 m,
written to `lab-box.json` and set as the lab scene's `voxelWorld.bounds` (nothing streams, cooks or simulates past it;
the far view shows sky there). The recipe keeps only the features reaching the box and is PROVED bit-identical to live
inside it (height, density, splat, biome, zone on a 4 m grid + every cell's generated doc; a difference puts the dropped
kinds back, never a tolerance); the scene keeps the globals and what stands in the box, the player moved onto it.
`--whole-world` makes the old full copy. `--lab <name>` gets its own world id. zonegen `concept` crops its bare render the same way.

## Layout (the owner's composition)

Knobs (`--set`) cannot carry a composition: the Tinkers' Camp passed every measured gate and was RETURNED because the
builder decided where things went (TOOLS-LAYOUT 2026-10-24). The owner writes `<job>/layout.json` (schema
`placeLayoutSchema` in `tools/_place-archetype.mts`, every field described):

```
{ "version": 1, "frame": "place", "origin": [x, z],
  "pieces": [{ "id", "element", "kind": "structure"|"prop"|"fire"|"decal", "prefab", "at": [x, z], "yaw"?, "scale"?, "pocket"?, "role"?, "landmark"?, "over"?: [ids], "on"?: "<earlier piece id>", "sink"?: m (a decal: below the ground; a piece `on` a host: set into its top), "quest"?: "<slot>" }],
  "roads"?: [{ "id", "element"?, "points": [[x, z], ...], "width"?: 7, "maxGrade"?: 0.12, "surface"?: "<recipe surface>", "grade"?: { "forecourt"?: 10, "past"?: 0, "width"?, "forecourtWidth"?, "level"?: 0, "feather"?: 10, "maxCut"?: 3, "maxFill"?: 2 } }],   // carved + painted roads inside the place (an avenue to a stair): `road-pb-<poi>-<id>`, graded like the approach track, never a ribbon
  "views":  [{ "name", "camera": [x, y, z], "target": [x, y, z] }],
  "ground": [{ "id", "element"?, "kind": "wear"|"mud"|"ash"|"paving"|"rubble", "at": [x, z], "radius"?, "texture"? }],
  "waters": [{ "id", "element"?, "on": "<piece id>", "marker"?: "basin-water", "material", "depth"?, "swim"? }],
  "areas":  [{ "id", "role", "at": [x, z], "radius", "note"?, "reach"?: [x, z] }] }
```

Pier pieces: a layout piece may carry `y` (WORLD height of its origin), held exactly with no seat and no solver. WET STRUCTURES ONLY (a pier deck run, a T-head, a jetty, a landing stage on their own driven posts, `wet: true`): every section keeps one deck level whatever the bed under it; the planner refuses `y` on a dry piece. The `seated` gate still measures each base outline against the bed: a post foot ending above the bed (the bed drops more than the post length under the deck) fails, so end the pier or put the T-head there. L (o-sunwine2 2026-10-31).

Cave pieces: a layout piece standing INSIDE a layout cave (a pillar, a platform, cargo in a `hall`) names it with `cave: "<cave id>"` and carries `y` (the world height of its origin on the carved floor: the cave operator report's `hall.floorY`, plus the prefab's origin height over its mesh bottom). The plan reads the ground before the lab carves the cave (the cliff over it), so such a piece is held, never seated; a cave piece with no `y`, or naming no layout cave, is refused. It is tagged `cave:<id>` and the `seated` gate measures it on the floor under its own height (`walkGroundFrom`, tools/_ground.mts), never the cliff top. L (o-sunwine3 2026-10-31).

Caves: `"caves": [{ "id", "entry", "direction"?, "length", "radius", "chamber"? | "hall"?, "wall"?, "floor"?, ... }]` compile to the core `cave` operator (`pb-<poi>-cave-<id>`).

The walk gate reaches a cave on the floor its operator carved: the lab lays a walked route `cave-<id>` (handoff `navigation.caves` -> `caveWalkRoute`) from the nearest point of the place's other routes, through the cave's `walkVia` ground points (place frame; route it round sheds and stacks), to just outside the mouth, along the bore floor the operator chose and, with a `hall`, to the hall centre. The ground planner never enters a cave: it reads the cliff top over it.

A cave a player walks into a built room through takes `"flatFloor": true` (the operator's level bore: grade 0 and a flat box passage per bore step, the hall on the same datum). Without it the mesher draws a round bore's bottom up to ~1.6 m over the field floor and a ~35 deg ramp down into the hall (Sunwine, o-sunwine4/5). A camera inside a cave is a layout view with `"inside": true` (its y is over the pad, never raised over the rock above it).

| Cave end | What it makes | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `chamber: r` | a remove blob centred 0.75 r over the bore-end floor, about 1.2 r across: its floor is a BOWL 0.25 r deep | a natural den, a nest, loose cargo |
| `hall: { width, length, height, roofRise, roofNoise, wallNoise }` (o-sunwine2 2026-10-31, Derek: the hold must hold stonework and a temple) | an elliptical `passages` chamber with a FLAT floor at the bore-end floor, axis-aligned to the bore's last heading, joining the bore 4 m before its end; cover-checked by spheres under the vault (thin rock is refused), skinned by `wall`/`floor` | a room for built pieces: ashlar platforms, pillar rows, a dais. Default-off; a `chamber` wins when both are given |

- **A graded avenue** (road `grade`, `surface`): `worldgen road` follows the ground, so a crest mid-avenue stays (Red Dial: cut 0.14 m) and hides the stair foot. `grade` writes ONE masked height patch `pb-<poi>-grade-<id>` (cut and fill): a straight grade from the natural ground at the first point to a level forecourt (ground at the last point + `level`) for `forecourt` m before the end and `past` m beyond it (under a plinth), `width` wide (`forecourtWidth` at the forecourt), smoothstep-feathered `feather` m. It goes on the ground BEFORE the pieces seat (the plan runs again on it) and leads world-ops (seat pads win). `LAYOUT:` faults: a grade over `maxGrade`, a cut/fill under the band over `maxCut`/`maxFill`. `surface` paints the tread with a named recipe surface (a zone remaps the default dirt to its own ground: the avenue read as the field); then `lips` and `seated` as for any patch.
- **Sown fields** (`crops`, a farm; o-farms 2026-11-02, Marrow's Farm read as a small brown patch on a big plain): `{ id, element, at, size: [across, along], yaw, strips, furrow, sown?, bare?, layers?, tilled?, stooks? }` in the place frame. `plan` (`expandCrops`) writes the SOWN strips as crop polygons to `<job>/fields.json` (ids `<poi>-<crop>-<k>`, owner the poi; build removes the place's old fields, then upserts), paints the tilled field as ONE ground polygon (`<crop>-till`; with no `tilled` the layout `wear` contrast rule picks the surface) and then EACH STRIP as its own polygon over it (`<crop>-strip-<i>`: sown = the standing crop, stubble = reaped straw, bare = dark fresh-turned earth; `paint` names the surfaces, else the first of `CROP_SURFACES` in the palette, on Redstrand redstrand-oasis-grass / drygrass / redstrand-oasis-ground), lays stook rows on the reaped strips (`<crop>-stook-<k>`, bedded props), walls the field with a low dry-stone dyke in the place's stone family (`edge`, default 1.0 m, a 4 m gate on the side nearest the yard: a core wall line `<crop>-dyke` in `layout.walls`; `edge: null` when the brief already walls it) and paints a 2.6 m track from that gate to the yard edge (`<crop>-track`, `track: false` to skip). The crop COVER (`layers`, fields.json) draws only within ~50 m of the camera; the strip paint is what reads from 150 m and from above (o-fields 2026-11-02: four farms' fields read as one pale rectangle each from the iso pair). Ground entries take `poly` (place-frame polygon) for any cornered paint. Farms use the `farmstead` archetype (farm-goods and work-gear filler, yard density 1.6, wheeled carts denied, `read.ratio` 1: house and outbuildings read as one cluster). A dyke's track that crosses a brief yard wall needs a gate in that wall (data: split the line).
- **Extra dressing areas** (`areas`): a midden, an opened tomb, beyond the pockets' own areas (a pinpoint has one pocket). Each joins
  handoff siteDressing.areas, gets its own fresh dresser and plan `dressing/<poi>-<id>.json`, which `plan` keeps; `reach` is the
  walk gate's point at the area's edge (default the centre, where its props stand).
- **Per-place tweaks of a catalogued piece** (piece `overrides`): prefab-instance overrides, e.g. a pyre whose light burns at night
  only: `[{ "path": "bonfire/components/prefab/overrides", "value": [{ "path": "glow/components/light/when", "value": "night" }] }]`.
- **A door's portal** (`portals`: `{ id, on, at: [x,y,z], size: [w,h,d], scene, anchor, name, refusal }`): a walk-through `portal` (trigger) as a CHILD of the structure piece `on` names, at `at` in that prefab's own frame (+Z its front), so the solver's seat carries it; `scene: ""` is a SEALED door (the dungeon project does not exist yet). Then `portal-veil` and `portal-cover --fit` on the lab scene (o-gates 2026-10-30: a Codex gate block's door had no way to carry its portal through a layout). An open door also names its RETURN anchor (`returnAnchor: { id, at }`, `at` in the same host frame, on the sill or podium before the door): the place builds it, so a re-install keeps it (i-moon 2026-11-02: a re-install had dropped a hand-added return anchor).
- **Cut into a cliff** (placeRule `seat: "set-in"`): a front whose back runs into a face (a temple front, a tomb door) stands on its LOWEST ground; `foot` lifted a 38 m gate onto the rock behind it, `embed` sank it 15 % (o-gates 2026-10-30).
- **Bedded on purpose** (layout piece `bedded: true`): a piece SET INTO a slope or a cliff by design (backing rock filling behind a temple front, a ruin house cut into the hillside) is exempt from the usable-slope refusal; its seat and foundation take the fall, and the solver's own slope tolerance still refuses what it cannot seat. A placeRule `bedded` piece may intersect placed structures (Derek 2026-10-30, the Weeping Sun: "it needs to go into the mountain"). A bedded piece sinks to its LOWEST ground like a rock (the uphill side buried), and the float gate holds its mesh base at or under that ground.
- **Stairs cut into the ground** (layout `stairs[]`: points foot → top in the place frame, width, materials): a wall operator `variant: ramp` (`pb-<poi>-stair-<id>`) whose cut lowers the bank over the deck and whose steep stretches become 0.3 m flights between kerbs; its centre line joins the navigation network so the walk gate climbs it. Use it for a stairway up a slope (a ruin above a road); never a stair prop standing on the ground.
- **Seated measures buried footings**: a structure whose mesh bottom is under the -0.3 m band (a kit ruin with a 0.6 m footing) is outlined at its own bottom; a structure with no outline at all is a seated FAULT, never a silent pass (o-gates 2026-10-30: the sun-ruins pieces floated through on "no base outline").
- **Water in a built basin** (`waters`): one still, level sheet at a placed piece's marker (a kit basin's `basin-water` in its
  `<model>.markers.json`: centre, level and size), nothing moving; `pools` carve terrain and cannot fill a mesh basin.
- **Frame**: the place frame `views` use: origin the plan centre (`reports/plan.json` `centre`), +z toward the approach,
  +x its right, y over padY. `origin` pins the core there, so no later site search moves the composition.
- **Exact or refused**: `plan` places every piece at its `at`/`yaw`, settled to the ground there (structures and props
  through the structure path: seat, skirt, cover clearing, keep-clear; a fire is the fire slot; a decal lies at ground
  height and is never a solid). A piece that overlaps another (yawed footprints from the catalogue `dressing.size` x
  scale), stands in water, on ground steeper than `USABLE_SLOPE` (0.6), outside the reserved ground, or names an
  uncatalogued prefab or a non-vfx fire is a `LAYOUT:` fault in `plan.json` `faults[]` naming the piece (exit 1). Nothing
  is moved. `over: [ids]` exempts those pairs (both directions): a gantry straddling the bell, a tarp over a crate.
- **Quest pieces**: `quest: "<slot>"` puts that quest slot's prop (the `quest.<slot>=` set, else the piece's prefab) at
  the piece's `at`/`yaw` as a fixed floor item (`quest.entity`) of the core plan, never at the auto spot; it is not also built.
- **Hero props made at size**: a codex-craft piece scaled over x1.2 (`CODEX_SCALE_MAX`) WARNs `made too small: re-request
  at <m> m`; `props request add --size <m>` stores the height and the Codex brief says it is made at that size.
- **The layout is the whole composition**: the archetype's structures, its `read.beacon` (and the silhouette tower)
  and its pockets' listed `dressing.props` are not added. The archetype still gives terrain (pocket fits, pads), walls,
  clearings, encounters, the quest slots and the FILLER, which treats every piece's footprint as occupied ground. A pocket
  named by pieces (`pocket`) is centred on them. Layout `views` replace the `views` set (contract.json views still win). The painting's cameras are never copied by hand: `concept-<view>` shots come from the concept-on-site record (`<zone>/concept/<poi>.site.json` `renders`, world space); a layout view of that name more than 5 m off is a `LAYOUT:` fault (Red Dial 2026-10-30: hand-copied world offsets read in the turned place frame put the far camera 134 deg off).
- **Walls share the frame**: under a layout the core pocket's `walls` (`<pocket>.walls` sets) stand at their written `at` /
  `points` in the same place frame as the pieces; the tier spread (`spreadToFloor`, floor / fit) is undone for them, so a
  chapel drawn at [28,-4] beside a bell at [16,-2] is 12 m from it, not 35 (Exiles' Bell 2026-10-24).
- **Wet ground**: a piece whose footprint touches wet ground is refused unless it says `wet: true` (drowned steps
  running into a loch, a pier, a reed clump). The flag is a statement of intent, not a fix: a hall marked wet still
  floods. Seating is unchanged (the piece seats on the ground under it, under the water).
- **Lift and the landmark**: a piece's `lift` (metres) stands it above its seat — raised foundations for a kit building
  whose floor slab is at y=0 (0.6 m like town buildings; Ashen Vow 2026-10-24: the slab on the pad was a 410 m² z-fight).
  The archetype schema still wants exactly one landmark BEFORE the layout applies, so a set keeps a placeholder (the fire's
  default, or a wall's); when a layout piece carries `landmark`, every archetype wall's landmark is dropped and the piece
  is the one the silhouette rule and the approach read judge.
- **Coverage** (checked in `plan`, where the catalogue is at hand): every design.json element made of a catalogue prop
  has a piece or a ground patch, every piece names an element (`element <id> has no layout piece`,
  `piece <id> names unknown element <e>`). structure:/creature:/terrain:/decal:/water:/text:/light: entries are exempt,
  and so is an element whose props are all the place's quest props (the dressing plan places the quest entity).
- **Ground wear**: each `ground` entry is a groundPaints disc (the pocket `floor` path), surface by kind from
  `WEAR_SURFACES` (`<world>-<zone>-road` / `-ground` / `dirt` for wear, `-mud` / `mud`, `-ash` / `ash`, `-paving` /
  `paving` / `cut-stone`) or `texture`; a kind the recipe has no surface for is a refusal naming it. Every disc is at
  least 4 m radius (`GROUND_MIN_RADIUS_M`, default 4) with a 1 m feather; `wear` takes the DARKEST candidate (zone skin ground,
  road, accent) that still differs from the open ground (skin grass / ground) by `WEAR_CONTRAST_MIN` (25) luma, else the one that
  differs most (Hagsheer: its ground, not the pale gravel road; r1c 2026-10-31: on Redstrand the skin ground is 5 luma off the
  grass and four camps showed no ground change in the batch iso frames, so wear is the accent dust there); `plan` WARNs any
  disc (not ash) under the floor: "it does not read from above". Zone tokens (`zone:wear`) use the same rule. A camp's
  wear should cover the whole camp (radius 15-20 m for a small camp), not only the fire; `rubble` is 6-10 catalogued `rock-kit/boulder-s-*` stones in
  the disc (`slot:rubble`), skinned by rock-skins.
- **Before a chain**: `$T props <world> --zone <z> --location <id>` renders every piece (the props proof soft renderer,
  no browser) into `reports/layout-props.png` with its id, its element's `shows` and its catalogued size, and prints the
  size-band WARN (fire > 3 m, prop > 6 m, structure < 1 m; also `WARN:` notes in `plan.json`). Look at it first.

## The Red Dial standard (2026-10-30)

Derek: "Red Dial is the best POI you have created to date; it should be the gold standard moving forward." The recipe for
a grand ruin (a city, a temple precinct, a fallen capital), in order. Each step names the tool; nothing is placed by hand.

1. **Concept first, measured through the painting's camera.** Codex paints the far and approach frames over the true
   render (`concept/<poi>.site.json` keeps each camera). Mark each hero piece's pixel box and the painted ground outline in
   `<job>/concept-marks.json` (`extent`: anchor, outline, masses). `place-build scale` projects the built pieces through
   the same camera: pieces and the district's area and width must each come within 15 % of the painting.
2. **A layout script, not a grid.** One `mklayout.mjs` in the job writes `layout.json` (place frame): bent streets along
   the fall, lots laid along them with their own gap, setback and a 5-15 deg turn, walled compounds, gates closing street
   ends, towers on the skyline the painting shows. Catalogue footprints come from `dressing.size`, re-read whenever the
   kit is re-exported. `place-build plan` refuses overlaps, steep or wet ground and stretched pieces before any build.
3. **A WFC ruin kit family for the buildings.** Houses, walls, towers, arcades and gates come from one kit style
   (`sun-ruins/*`: building-constructor + the WFC ruin layer). Ruins keep no upper floors, roofs or loose debris (R76),
   and the kit's support gate refuses any floating island at export. The placement solver grounds each shell from its
   `placeRule` (grade 0.3 on a raised floor) and skirts it with a foundation; a layout never carries a hand `lift`.
4. **Rubble heaps by name.** Debris is catalogued heaps (`sun-ruin-rubble/heap|heap-long|spill|mound` and their
   `-blocks` variants), mixed, a minority with blocks. Declare layout `areas` at the streets and compounds, and run one
   fresh Sonnet dresser per area (`site-sockets`, `props menu`, `dress check`), one at a time. Never generated debris.
5. **Fires are prefabs on markers.** Every tower, gate and pillar fire is one of two wrapper prefabs
   (`city-fires/city-beacon`, `city-beacon-cap`): size, night-only light, glow and reach live in those two files. A fire
   names its host's `marker` (`fire-1`), never copied coordinates; the `support` gate fails any overhang or clip.
6. **Light by the wash gate.** Big lights burn at night only, glow at most 1, no smoke. `place-build shots` runs with
   `--wash` and the `wash` gate judges every frame (luma band, veil share, far fires read by their flame).
7. **Far view first.** Judge `concept-far` before anything near: the city must read at 450 m by its extent and its fires.
   Then the approach, then the streets at player height, at 12:00 and 20:00, on one side-by-side sheet against the
   painting. Report draw calls from the far frame (over ~300: an HLOD proxy). Paint the far frame from where the ground really
   shows the place: Red Dial's far camera stands 20 m up inside a woodland edge, and the woodland hides most of it at
   player height (a vista clearing of 44 trees was refused; the far-fire row is excepted, quality-exceptions.json).
8. **Gates, then a fresh reviewer.** `place-build gates` (all rows, including `scale`, `support` and `wash`), the sheet,
   then a fresh grey-box reviewer before detail work.

## Placement solver (2026-10-30)

**Agents state intent (what, roughly where, facing); the placement solver owns exact transforms. A gate that catches a
placement fault means the solver is missing a rule: fix the solver, never the place by hand. A repeated visual is one
prefab with shared parameters.**

One solver in `@hitreg/core` (`packages/core/src/place-solver/`, headless and deterministic) is used by every placer:
place-build layout pieces and the lab re-solve, `dress apply` (site dressing), `settle` (stilt structures, judged), the town
builder (`town-settle` floors) and statues (`placeRule` written by the statue maker). The tools share one wrapper,
`tools/_place-solve.mts` (prefab size/footing/markers, the scene's obstacles and keep-outs, one ops batch back).

| Part | What it does | Data it reads |
|---|---|---|
| Seat | samples the footprint (corners, edge mids, centre, or the base outline); `foot` stands the piece flush on the highest ground, sunk at most its catalogue `footing`; `plinth` levels to the lowest; `embed` beds a heap; `float-ok` keeps y. Too steep or too deep: tries XZ moves up to `tolerance.move` and yaw ±`tolerance.yaw`, else DROPS with the reason | `placeRule.seat/grade/tolerance`, `dressing.footing` |
| Foundation | where the ground falls past a `foundation` piece's footing, a skirt in its own masonry runs down to the lowest ground +0.3 m: one static mesh + trimesh collider per piece (`<id>--foundation`, tag `foundation-of:<id>`). A piece with a floor (grade > 0) never sinks into the high ground and its skirt has no top (the floor covers it); an open piece's skirt is capped over all the ground under it (a platform), never in the ground's or its own planes. The seated gate reads the skirt as solid and a solver piece's footing as its base; the float gate leaves a founded piece to the seated gate | `placeRule.foundation {material, depth, inset, tile}` (sun-ruins, strand-town, pedestal statues) |
| No intersecting | footprint OBB against every solid structure's SOLID cells (its walls and piers on a 0.5 m plan grid; an open court's yard is free): pushed out by the least penetration, else dropped. `bedded` pieces are exempt | structure prefabs ≥ 6 m², `solidCells` |

| Keep-outs | door clear zones 1.5 m deep × opening width + 0.4 m (both faces), stair approaches, hearth clearances, lanes; anything placed in one moves out or drops | WFC `door` / stair / hearth markers (`export_building.py` writes `door` markers since 2026-10-30; `--markers-only` re-writes them without a GLB), `placeRule.openings`, layout lanes |

## Lifts (a structure kind beside a long descent)

A flight of steps down a 90 m cliff takes minutes; a winch LIFT (Thunder Bluff) runs beside it forever on the shared
clock (the `lift` builtin: a kinematic deck that carries its riders and is never replicated). Tool: `tools/_place-lift.mts`.

```
$T lift plan <world> --zone <z> --location <place> --lift <id> --top x,z --bottom x,z [--facing deg] [--exit front|back] [--top-y y] [--bottom-y y]
$T lift gate <world> --zone <z> --location <place> --lift <id>              # a real body rides down and back up on the server's stepping
$T lift install|uninstall <world> --zone <z> --location <place> --lift <id>  # refused without a passing gate on the current ops
```

- top = bottom XZ is a plumb shaft (give `--facing`, out from the cliff); a bottom further out is an INCLINED winch (the
  deck stays level). A 70 degree slope has no room for a plumb shaft: `SHAFT` says where the line meets the ground.
- The headframe stands on the ground BEHIND the top deck, never over the drop; when the deck hangs clear of the edge a
  gangway spans the gap (two 3 m pieces; `EDGE` past 5.6 m). The landing frame stands at the bottom exit (`--exit back` = a cave mouth
  under the top). Both sit on their LOWEST ground (never float) and refuse more than 2 m of rise under them.
- The plan reads the scene's colliders too: a wall, kerb, pier or flight in a rider's volume on either deck, in the shaft,
  on the walk on/off or under a frame is `STRUCTURE` (kirk-stair 2026-10-21: a bell-cote wall stopped the ride gate's body
  after the plan said ok). `--shaft` carves a straight box round the deck (`lift-<id>-shaft`, exempt from the 6 m rule
  like bridge cuts: the ride gate proves it). The cradle and rope are written with mesh overrides (`moving`, not
  `static`/`hlod`) so they follow the deck on a client. `tools/lift-play.mts` is the real-client ride + pictures.
- **A place reached BY a lift** (a cove under a sea cliff, o-coast sunwine-hold 2026-10-31): layout `arrival { at, by: "lift:<id>" }` starts the approach leg, its track and the walk route at the lift's bottom landing instead of the nearest road (plan NOTE `approach: arrives by lift:<id>`). The grey-box evidence is then the `lift gate` (the ride) plus the walk gate (landing to core). A lift-only place still wants a walking trail from the network: log it for the road owner.
- Files: `<place job>/lifts/<id>.{spec,ops,plan,gate,install-inverse}.json`. Entities are tagged `lift:<id>`, not
  `poi:<place>`, so a place re-install never takes the lift with it.

## Bridges (a structure kind across a gorge)

A hero crossing (the Lych Span, HERO-BRIDGE 2026-10-21) is a place whose landmark IS the structure: `place-build bridge`
reads `<job>/bridge.json` and writes the job files the rest of the tool uses (then `lab`, `build`, `gates`, `shots`,
`install` as for any place). Tool: `tools/_place-bridge.mts` (schema `bridgeSchema`, every field described; test
`test/place-bridge.test.ts`).

```
$T bridge <world> --zone <z> --location <place> [--spec <file>]   # <job>/bridge.json { bridge, roads, views }
```

- `bridge`: the deck line (`from` = a gate tower's centre or the first end, `to` = the far end, one level `y`), piers
  (centre, foot and top sizes, set-back heights, cutwater side, half-round refuge), `arches.springing` (all spans equal:
  one crown height), start/end kinds (`gate-tower` with its round-arched passage on the deck line, `block`, `rim`) and a
  material per role (face, arch, parapet, coping, deck, footing, roof). It compiles to ONE `landmark` operator
  (`kind: built`, `pb-<place>-<id>`): bevelled boxes for tiers, deck, parapets and the tower; closed prisms with UVs laid
  flat per face for the arch rings, spandrel bays, refuges, cutwaters and the roof, so every face (caps, soffits, the
  deck's underside) is at 40 tx/m with no off-axis stretch. Bevels stay small (a chamfer strip takes box UVs off its axis).
- Seating: every pier footing, impost block, abutment, the tower and the band beyond each end arch is a BASE buried
  `bury` m under the lowest ground of its outline (min of the heightfield and the surface cast); an end arch whose outer
  springer stands over lower ground gets an impost block run into the wall until the ground reaches the springing. Gate
  `seated` reads `reports/bridge.json` bases (daylight along each outline <= 0.05 m).
- Roads: each `roads[]` entry is a carved + painted recipe road (`worldgen road`, `road-pb-<place>-<id>`) from a deck end
  (`from: start|end`) through its points, pinned to the deck height at the deck (`--pin-start`): flush, no ramp edge.
  Gate `roads` checks every one (handoff `approachTracks`).
- Routes: `deck` (the gate passage and the deck, `onBuilt`: the lab build keeps its heights) and one per road.
- A gate tower's passage gets a masked LOWERING patch (`pb-<place>-gate`, `lowerPatch`): the mouth flush with the deck,
  under the passage paving 5 cm below it (the ground poked through, then lay coplanar with the paving: zfight); samples
  already lower keep weight 0, so the rim's edge is never filled (a level patch there was a 12 m lip).
- Reach targets: the deck start only. poi-walk walks the `deck` route; its ground planner cannot chain landmark targets
  along a deck 100 m over a gorge (STUCK at once).
- `views[]` take `camera` + `target` (a contract shot list): `light-shots` holds the rig off and puts the camera there.

## Structural fit: place = fit (2026-11-10)

Anything that holds something up or runs between two things is placed ONLY by fitting it to the real colliders. That
covers shoring, A-frames, raking shores, ladders, chutes, walkways, cantilevered and scaffold decks, and hanging chains.
Owner rule: a frame that carries nothing is a fault. One tool does this, `tools/structure.mts`, with one contact solver,
`tools/_contact.mts`. The spider-web planners (world-kit `spider-webs/skirts.mts`) prove their anchors with the same
solver.

- **Contacts are data.** A structural catalogue row declares `job.kind` and `job.contacts`: local points, each with what
  it must touch.
  - What a contact can touch: `floor`, `roof`, `rock-face`, `wall`, `ledge-top`, `support`, `support-edge` or
    `roof-or-beam`.
  - The structural kinds are carries-roof, braces-wall, spans, leans-on-wall, cantilevers and hangs.
  - The desert-ogre kit writes contacts for every piece, fitted pieces included.
  - A contact is met when it sits at most 5 cm short of a collider surface or at most 10 cm into it.
  - Colliders are the baked rock shell, terrain and every other placed object.
  - There is no bounds or floating-support fallback.
- **Place = fit.** Run `npx tsx tools/structure.mts fit <project> --spots spots.json --apply <tag> --out report.json`.
  Each spot is `{ id, piece, at, yaw, search?, width?, height? }`.
  - **`piece: "shoring"` is stretched to the site.**
    - Rays find the rock walls on either side at 1.6 m up. The posts stand 0.15 m off the walls (no slot behind a post), and the frame centres
      between them.
    - Rays find the floor under each post, and the roof every 0.5 m across the span.
    - The kit fitter (world-kit desert-ogre-kit `build.mjs fit shoring`) rebuilds the frame to those measures:
      - the posts are cut to the floor;
      - the cap sits under the lowest roof;
      - the cribbing is stacked and wedged up to the roof at each crib.
    - The allowed range is 3.6 to 10 m wide and 4 to 13 m high.
  - **A fixed catalogue piece** (a walkway, a deck, the stairs) is tried at the spot, then at offsets of 0.4 to 1.2 m
    and at headings of plus or minus 10 and 20 degrees.
  - **Reject, don't fudge.** A spot whose contacts do not ALL land is not written, and neither is one that would
    stretch out of range. The report says which contact missed and by how much.
  - `--apply <tag>` replaces that tag's entities in one applyOps batch, so the step is idempotent.
- **The gate.** `npx tsx tools/structure.mts scene <project> --out ...` re-proves every placed structural piece against
  the CURRENT colliders.
  - Run it after any shell rebuild: when the rock moves, the stage that placed a piece must re-fit it.
  - Any unmet contact fails the gate.
  - Pieces with no catalogue job but `dressing.structure` or a `structure:support|span` tag are checked by geometry: a
    support's top and feet bands, or both ends of a span.
- **Not stretched yet: ladders, chutes and chains.** Their kit fitters exist (`fit ladder --length`,
  `fit chute --head --setback`), but they are not wired to ray measurement yet. Until they are, either place them as
  fixed fitted pieces through `fit`, where they are proved and rejected like any other piece, or leave them out.
