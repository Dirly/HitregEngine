# MMO POI scale and supported dressing

Use these HitReg authoring targets to choose a site before shaping it. They are sizing variants, not automatic generation quotas. The user can request another scale. Measure the playable space, not the bounding box of the terrain edit.

| Tier | Usable-area scale | Intended experience | Terrain work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pinpoint | Object or compact discovery footprint; no grove-size quota | Cached crate, camp on the hill, Seven Whispers, a single interaction | Prefer natural ground; footing adjustments only. A heightmap is usually unnecessary. |
| Small | One grove; Silkroot Hollow is the current small reference, not medium | A local adventure with connected encounter pockets and room for movement | Local heightmap or terrain edits where useful, with an understandable approach and exit. |
| Medium | Roughly 2× the agreed small grove's usable area | More exploration and encounters between distinct pockets | Plan connected space before dressing; preserve retreat routes. |
| Large | Roughly 2× medium (4× small) in usable area | Major zone destination with multiple encounters and a recognisable silhouette | Deliberate landscape plan; distant, middle-distance and player-height views required. |

These tiers replace the earlier numeric bands, which understated MMO space. Silkroot currently measures about 1,573 m² of connected terrain; Derek considers it small and wants a further doubling later. That expansion is deferred, not already built. Before another size-dependent build, state the chosen small reference in square metres: current Silkroot versus an approved enlarged version. Scale medium and large from that same reference. Doubling means usable area, not doubling both horizontal dimensions. Do not enlarge a pinpoint cache merely to fill a band.

Dungeon presence is separate from POI size: small, medium and large sites can each contain a tasteful dungeon entrance, or remain entirely outdoors. Size never implies a portal. Survey existing dungeon approaches and their travel catchments before adding another; justify spacing through zone pacing, story and discovery. Keep the newbie zone sparse in major dungeon destinations instead of filling every POI or road bend with one. A medium site can remain concealed; a large site should have a deliberate silhouette.

## Measure usable space

- Record tier, editable footprint, actual usable area, connected routes, and encounter pockets separately. Report dimensions in metres and area in square metres. State whether a requested multiplier means length or area; four times area means roughly twice each horizontal dimension.
- Count connected, reachable ground with a declared slope limit (18° is the default combat-space measurement). Exclude walls, steep embankments, inaccessible shelves and the incoming travel spur. Subtract substantial tree trunks, rocks and dressing footprints. Use the same measurement before and after a resize.
- Reserve combat/movement pockets before dressing. Start around 20–30m clear diameter for a small MMO pack, then check the actual mobs' radii, aggro, leash, ranged attacks and player/group space. Capacity is a design claim until real spawns are tested; do not describe empty pockets as populated encounters.
- Link pockets with 4–6m usable paths; let players see the next choice, retreat and move around other players. Flat area alone is not enough: check that the whole progression is connected, not merely that the entrance is reachable.
- Include procedural scatter in the live check. Reserve local travel clearances before calling a floor usable; an authored-tree-only review misses generated rocks and trunks. Drive the game's actual controller for the live walkthrough. Do not substitute a kinematic movement API on a dynamic player body and treat the result as gameplay evidence.
- Vegetation frames and partitions the site. Use seeded local scatter and density controls for ordinary grove dressing; hand-place only specific anchors or landmarks. Preserve combat discs and route clearance. Use current shared tree assets as instances; a tree art pass is a separate concern. Revalidate attachments after an asset's scale, pivot or mesh changes.
- Before accepting scaled trees, sample the actual final terrain triangles across the full transformed Bark/root footprint, including perimeter quarter points and interior points; center samples and raw heightmaps can miss open bases on ridges. Keep visible tree and Bark collider transforms paired. Lower on a moderate slope; relocate if contact would bury low foliage. Fixed trees carrying attachments keep their attachment transforms; repair their base with a base-only extension when required. Record per-tree before/after gaps and an inverse, and carry approved placement transforms into the generator replay.
- A grove or forest mix should use the available compatible tree meshes and size variants, not repeat one representative mesh everywhere. Declare the biome mix and weights; vary species, silhouette, age/size and suitable foliage colors. Random scale alone does not substitute for model variation. When splitting a scatter rule into variants, preserve its total density budget and gates, check combined spacing, and validate each model's bark collision, wind material roles and near/far appearance.
- Match replacement vegetation to the established trees' world-space texel density, measured separately for bark and foliage using their UVs, image dimensions and placed scale. Nearest-neighbor sampling alone does not set pixel size. Preserve source images, downsample each role with nearest-neighbor before atlas packing, and record the reference and resulting density. Regenerate affected LOD/impostor textures and verify close and distant views. Keep species appropriate to the biome: autumn broadleaves are not a jungle or palm substitute. Scatter stumps and fallen wood sparsely with ground support and travel clearance.
- Match the established vegetation shading as well as its textures. Compare cutout mode, material settings, normal treatment, upward bias and brightness against an existing tree or ground-cover reference under the same light. Verify the runtime normals were actually processed: the current foliage-normal pass only accepts alpha-cutout materials, and assumes geometry-local Y is up. Blended imports can bypass the pass; rotated asset nodes can turn its upward bias sideways. Resolve those asset conventions before accepting trees, bushes or ferns, and rebuild their LOD/impostor representations from the corrected setup.
- LOD switching alone is not performance acceptance. Before a vegetation replacement is accepted, capture a fresh profiler sample and compare original versus replacement vegetation at the same camera, viewport, quality, population and warmed streaming state. Record actual per-frame draw calls (not cumulative renderer calls), submitted triangles, active model/material batches, frame p50/p95, and CPU/GPU/off-loop costs; keep startup/streaming costs separate. A larger catalog must not silently become every variant in every cell. Check co-visible variety and atlas-induced geometry growth against the baseline before expanding the live scatter mix.
- Measure tree heights in world metres against the player and nearby terrain before choosing a size mix. Preview a requested size change locally before changing every biome. Mix smaller growth with the main canopy and sparse landmark trees; a giant oak needs a broad crown and substantial trunk, not only a stretched sapling. Preserve existing dressing supports and route clearance when changing silhouettes. Scaling also changes texel density, collision and screen coverage: compensate bark UV repeats without unnecessary atlas seam cuts, and check foliage card size, overdraw and LOD under the final placed scale.
- A grove needs a perimeter screen beyond the usable clearing, not just trees lining its floor. Scatter a broad outer belt and check views toward exposed slopes from the entrance, inside, and outside. Record distribution around the perimeter; dense trees in one sector do not hide gaps elsewhere. Keep intentional approach openings. Trees supplement terrain integration; they do not excuse floating roots or visibly broken ground.
- Ground trees at their final placed scale against rendered terrain triangles across the actual basal rim, including edge and interior samples. Sampling only the pivot misses downhill gaps; requiring an upper trunk band to be buried gives false failures. Lower exposed bases with their matching colliders while preserving approved scale. Relocate steep placements when sinking would bury the canopy; preserve fixed dressing attachments and use a base-only repair where needed. Save the grounding audit, inverse and replay transforms, bind saved placements to terrain/model hashes, and re-audit after either changes. Verify ridge contacts in the rendered scene.
- Report a site's local tree count with its screened footprint, encounter-edge versus outer-screen distribution, and nearest-neighbour spacing; list procedural world vegetation separately. Check for redundant clumps across the combined placements before increasing density. Prefer a measured spacing pass that preserves supports and useful sightline coverage over accepting a large count just because it passes a sector-count check.
- Image-generated heights are a guide. Preserve the original; use calibrated metres, smoothing and bounded grade/level corrections. Check terrain seams and protected roads. A shaded image's dark bands and bright rims must not silently become trenches or spikes.

## Ceiling dressing must have physical support

For webs, drapes, banners, chains and hanging roots, record the supporting entity or terrain surface, the actual contact point and the attachment method for each load-bearing end. Air is not a support.

- A transparent image's rectangular corner is not necessarily a visible strand. Locate the opaque strand/cloth edge and bridge it to the support with visible silk, rope, chain or a modeled hem. Inspect the attachment close up; do not accept only a plane-corner or heightfield test.
- Use the final rendered/collision surface: raycast tree trunks or rock faces at the attachment's actual Y. Check a finite intersection on the named support, with a small embedded end (roughly 2–8cm). A rock's heightfield being above the endpoint does not prove contact with the visible wall.
- Tree attachments use the trunk/branch, never the bounding box or transparent leaf cards. Validate in the tree's local space and keep a visible wrap/knot/strand around it. If the tree sways, either make the attachment follow it or use a stable supporting trunk/rock.
- Prefer independent three-anchor triangular patches for this zone's web canopies. They can span cliffs across voxel-cell boundaries, trees, or mixed supports. Every tip needs a physical support and visible alpha coverage there. A quad is an option only after its distortion and edge treatment pass review. Do not add a thick continuous tube around the sheet: it reads as rope framing. Use fine silk only where an actual attachment gap needs bridging.
- For Silkroot-style nest webs, at least one vertex of every patch reaches actual ground. Use sloped sheets from cliff/tree supports to a ground contact near the clearing's edge, rather than a uniform sheet at head height. Verify the ground point with a downward ray on final terrain triangles and record its surface and embedding distance. Keep low sections outside travel corridors and encounter centres. This ground-vertex rule is for these nest webs, not a requirement that every banner or ceiling drape touches the floor.
- A numeric ground vertex alone is not visual acceptance. From normal player views the visible silk must read as reaching and spreading onto the ground, with an understandable supported connection. If it still reads as a suspended ceiling with a tiny dangling tip, revise the sheet layout/coverage even when the attachment check passes.
- Keep texels in world metres, independent of the span. Derive the target from an actual nearby material's tile size and image resolution, and validate both texture axes after transforms. Triangle geometry alone does not solve stretched UVs. Repeat appropriate artwork or use smaller supported patches; never fit the whole image once across an arbitrarily larger span. Inspect tiling and clipped silhouettes in-engine as well as checking the numeric scale. Heavy coverage comes from overlapping patches while retaining usable floor/camera clearance.
- Measure the lowest **visible** strand over walkable routes against the local floor. Default to at least 3m headroom (more for mounts/large mobs), or move the web outside the route. If intended as an obstacle, implement and test its collision/interaction rather than implying it visually.
- Check the actual third-person camera boom and sightline through the approach and encounter pockets. Capsule clearance alone does not prove that overhead dressing leaves the player a usable view.
- Keep non-solid webs out of collision, but include all supporting trunks, branches, rocks, egg sacs and solid props in the access review. Check approach, reverse exit and lateral lanes with the player's actual capsule, plus a live engine check including procedural vegetation.
- Reject floating support ends, buried invisible sheets, unexplained attachment to leaves, webs covering the camera, and silhouettes that disappear into adjacent rocks. Preserve close-up attachment and player-height evidence alongside the reviewer report.

## Enforce the measurable rules

Use `dressing-checks.mjs` from the authoring generator before installation. `checkTriangleTexels` accepts world-space triangle vertices, their UVs, texture pixel dimensions and a metres-per-texel target. It checks both singular values of the texture-to-world mapping, so an average that hides directional stretch cannot pass. A reasonable initial tolerance is10%; record the chosen reference and threshold. Apply it to all output triangles, after object scale.

For a requested grove screen, call `checkGrovePerimeter` with the local site centre, inner/outer screening radii and distribution target. Its default asks for at least two trees in nine of twelve sectors; choose site-appropriate exceptions for entrances, water or bare cliffs before authoring. Treat a failed check as a build failure. This proves distribution, not visual occlusion: still inspect the player-height perimeter and texture appearance. Support/clearance checks and actual traversal remain separate gates. Run `node --test tools/poi-review/dressing-checks.test.mjs` after changing these helpers.

## Structure the prefab for culling

A POI root (tagged `poi`) already hides as a unit behind terrain. Split the rest by what should arrive when (`docs/culling.md`): keep the landmark (tower, statue, walls, big tree) directly under the root so it reads from afar; put clutter and small props under a child with `culling: { minScreenPx: 6 }`; put furnishings and inner rooms under a child with `culling: { interior: true }`, raising `reveal` for wide doors. Interior subtrees never enter the distant HLOD bake.

## Structures and builder handoff

When the POI design calls for a fort, church or another building, request a focused builder agent using the tool-neutral `../MMO/WFC/wfc/README.md`, its generator rules and `../MMO/WFC/KIT-RULES.md`. The existing building-constructor skill wraps that same asset-construction workflow. Supply the building's purpose, measured footprint, floor/entry elevations, approach direction, material system and required interior connections. The builder returns generated assets and its geometry/visual review; the POI author owns terrain integration and combined player/camera access checks. Use `hitreg-town-authoring` for engine-side town placement and integration. Do not substitute an improvised DC building for available WFC assets, or invent a building simply because the handoff is available.
