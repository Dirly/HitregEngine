# Review the place against its brief

Use this for the early blockout/finish sample and the final read-only review. Inspect actual player-height views, overview, approach and functional routes. Return concrete failures and their images to the owner. The examples below describe these briefs; they are not mandatory features of every POI of that type.

## Questions the owner must resolve

- **Purpose and history:** Who built/uses/occupies this place, what do they do, what changed, and how can the player read that? Layout and condition should tell the same story. Repeating a fresh-dirt prop over every grave implies a new burial ground or mass casualty; it does not establish an old cemetery. Ruin/abandonment is more than bones scattered beside ordinary camp supplies.
- **Spatial logic:** Where do people and goods enter, work, gather and leave? A focal structure or landmark needs a relationship to the approach and the activities around it. Markers, crosses, basins, benches and cages need a legible use, bearing and position. A basin might belong at a chapel approach or washing area when the story calls for it; a randomly placed basin has no inferred purpose.
- **Edges and access:** Inspect boundaries against roads, paths, drains, cliffs and nearby structures in the actual game. A passing lane ray does not excuse an enclosure cutting across an established road. Check setbacks and travel width after the final boundary and vegetation are placed; purposeful road changes require the brief's authorization and a working replacement connection.
- **Asset choice and presentation:** Select by function and appearance rather than availability. Whole vehicles, appropriate buildings, fitted fence panels or generated artwork may communicate better than a convenient unrelated kit fragment. Openings face their users and goods; stalls need usable cover and service space. Read texture scale, silhouette and support from player height before repeating groups. Substantial custom buildings use the applicable builder workflow.
- **Activity and progression:** The requested safe activity or hostile adventure must exist at its requested extent. Threat identity, encounter placement, discoveries, branches and retreats need a rationale. A mob's presence does not by itself establish the right adversary, pacing or combat space. Required mechanics need actual behavior; name plates and quest-shaped props are insufficient.
- **Terrain and composition:** Does the clearing, gorge, shore or mountain still read as that landform? Broad earthworks need an engineered or landscape reason. A usable-area target is not a reason to turn an organic site into a rectangular pad. Preserve a deliberate silhouette and varied spaces, including breathing room and transitions, rather than filling a quota with props.

Record a reasoned outcome for these questions, against the brief's description and requirements. Area, route and asset counts support the assessment; they do not decide whether the place is believable. A user-rejected design is unfinished even when its numerical report passes.

## Example: an established cemetery beside a safe road

The [cemetery brief](cemetery.example.json) describes mature grave rows, a small chapel and a low wrought-iron boundary. Its owner should:

- Set the enclosure back from the road and connect a clear gate, chapel approach and burial aisles. Fit the boundary to the actual ground and preserve travel outside it.
- Give the chapel a measured interior and an appropriate WFC/building kit construction. Make its entrance and focal role visible. Put symbols and any requested basin where their burial/religious/work use is legible.
- Differentiate older settled graves from a few specified recent or disturbed plots through terrain, markers, vegetation and condition. A fresh cut has floor/edges, spoil and a way back out, not merely a coffin placed on grass.
- Choose a fence method that suits the art style. Image-generated alpha artwork on fitted planes can represent fine wrought-iron panels when appropriate, with separate posts/gates/supports and collision matching the intended boundary. A mesh may suit closer views or greater depth. The owner generates/intakes/catalogues the selected artwork and validates transparency, repeats, scale, joins, support and actual access. A fence texture is not proof of a working gate.
- Inspect a representative grave group, fence corner/gate and chapel approach before repeating the whole cemetery. Review the completed place's age, spacing, silhouette and relation to the road.

## Example: a large non-hostile meadow hub

Plan trade, packing, arrivals, residents and the requested hub interactions. Let the meadow shape the boundary; use level courts and individual footings where work needs them, linked by believable circulation. Covered produce stands, parked whole caravans, work/store buildings and sleeping areas need functional relationships. A larger grading rectangle plus more tents does not establish that organization. Test any required shops/quests rather than reporting them as future capacity.

## Example: a large mine through a gorge

Explicitly plan the described underground extent, route choices, destinations, elevations and role of the lower gorge. Preserve readable progression from safe work ground into the requested threat. Survey enclosure, volume and ground support before committing tunnels. The entrance is one connection in the network; finishing its portal and first chamber does not complete a large-mine brief. Report measured routes/areas and tested required behavior, without substituting the excavation's bounding box for playable space.
